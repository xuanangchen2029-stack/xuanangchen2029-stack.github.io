Current researchPROJECT 01

BEAMbot

Bistable mechanics meets electromagnetic actuation.

How can mechanical bistability and electromagnetic switching work together in modular robots? I’m developing compliant units and studying how structural geometry, magnetic placement, and drive signals shape their behavior.

  • Laser-cut PETG mechanisms
  • Force–gap and electrical characterization
  • Multi-unit prototypes and drive-electronics development
Inside the research +

The approach. Connect mechanical testing with coil characterization and switching experiments, then use that evidence to guide the next hardware iteration.

What comes next. Flexible-coil integration, compact local electronics, and richer unit-level motion are directions under investigation. Autonomous assembly and decentralized robot behavior remain longer-term questions.

Shown: wired prototypes used for mechanical and actuation experiments.

02 / How I work

Across disciplines.
Grounded in experiments.

My training bridges mechanical systems and electronics. I like research questions that require both—and a prototype that can put the model to the test.

01

Design the mechanics

Use geometry, material behavior, and compliant mechanisms to shape a robot’s motion.

CAD · Finite elements · Laser cutting · Rapid prototyping

02

Connect actuation & control

Bring the mechanism together with its actuator, drive electronics, and control signals.

Electromagnetics · Pneumatics · Embedded programming · PCB design

03

Measure, then iterate

Compare models with force, motion, and electrical measurements to decide what to change next.

Experimental characterization · MATLAB · Python · Data analysis

A question I want to pursue

How much of a robot’s adaptability can we design into its body—and how should sensing and control build on it?
